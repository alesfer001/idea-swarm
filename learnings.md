# Learnings

What has worked and what hasn't when taking ideas to real people. Unlike `rejected-ideas.md` (ideas the pipeline killed), this is about the founder: which channels and ways of reaching people actually fit. Update it after each marketing test.

## What's working

**Crypto ecosystem: CKB Kickstarter (CrowdCell)**
- Communicating through Telegram groups and forums.
- Asking for grants after showing something built, not before.
- Networking through Telegram and written messages.

**Pattern:** written, asynchronous channels in communities where I take part as a member, and asking only once there's something concrete to show.

## What's not working

**TradeSync: cold-call outreach to tradespeople**
- 5 people called:
  - 1 already uses a tool
  - 1 said he's too old to start using tools
  - 1 said to call him later
  - 2 refused to talk
- The clients may exist, but I have no way of knowing where they are or how to reach them. The only option left is calling tradespeople around me at random and hoping one has the right mindset and is kind enough to discuss a tool with a stranger.
- Marketing calls need a charisma and social relentlessness I don't have.

**Pattern:** voice and cold outreach, and audiences that don't gather anywhere I can reach in writing.

## What this means for new ideas

- The buyers must gather somewhere online that I can reach **in writing**: forums, subreddits, Telegram/Discord groups, marketplaces, search.
- Prefer audiences where I can be a genuine participant, and lead with something useful or built (a free version, a demo) before asking for anything.
- No idea whose first customers depend on phone calls, door-to-door or in-person sales.
- Be wary of offline, low-tech audiences (e.g. trades) even if the pain is real: being reachable matters as much as demand.
